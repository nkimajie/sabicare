# SabiCare: positioning and story

Everything we build, write and show should serve this.

## Positioning

**Built for Africa, starting in Nigeria, designed to scale to the world.**

> Most women who die in pregnancy and childbirth are in Africa, and Nigeria has more of these deaths than any other country. So we started where the need is greatest. SabiCare is built for Nigeria first, designed to scale across Africa, and then to any country where a family has to decide alone whether to go to hospital.

**Source for the first sentence:** the WHO maternal mortality fact sheet (dated 7 April 2025, https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/maternal-mortality) says: "Sub-Saharan Africa alone accounted for around 70% of maternal deaths (182 000)" in 2023. Open the page and confirm the quote yourself before using it in the video or on Devpost. The Nigeria part is backed by the README: about 75,000 deaths, 28.7% of the world total (UN MMEIG, 2025).

## Three stages

1. **Now: Nigeria.** English and Pidgin today; Hausa, Yoruba and Igbo next.
2. **Next: sub-Saharan Africa.** Swahili, French, Amharic and others; local emergency numbers.
3. **Vision: South Asia** and any country with high maternal deaths.

Stage 1 is built. Stages 2 and 3 are the plan, not built. Say "designed to scale", never "available across Africa".

## Why it scales

- The danger-sign rules are based on WHO guidance that applies in every country. Only the language layer, the emergency number and the clinic schedule change per country.
- The app runs in a phone browser and uses WhatsApp and SMS, which are common across Africa.

## The human story

Imagine Amina. She is an illustrative character, not a real person.

Amina is 34 weeks pregnant in Nigeria. At night she has a severe headache, swollen feet and blurred vision. Her family tells her to wait, because nobody knows these are danger signs.

With SabiCare she types "my head dey pain me well well and my eye dey blur". The screen turns red. It shows her why, in her own words. One tap alerts her husband on WhatsApp with the hospital, driver and blood donors from her birth plan.

The decision that used to take hours takes one minute.

Amina is in Nigeria. The same night is happening in Kenya, in Ethiopia, in Bangladesh.
